signals

overview

Every year, hundreds of thousands of Indian students move to a new city for college. They arrive with ambition, a packed bag, and no one who knows their name.

Signals is a mobile app for those first 90 days. It combines structured daily challenges with an anonymous campus feed, giving students a low-stakes way to act, and a real-time way to know they are not the only one struggling.

the problem

Nobody told their parents how they actually felt. They called home and said everything was fine.

the product

Three flows. One system. Every part connects to the others - intentionally.

Onboarding
Value before sign-up. The normalisation number appears before any account is created.
Daily Move
One move per day. Always free. A buddy doing the same thing removes the fear of going alone.
Signal Wall
Every card on the signal wall is something a real student felt that week. They read it. They tap "me too." The count goes up. No algorithm, just recognition.

what i learned
a note on tone

To find the right words for the users. With every screen, every label, every CTA, I had to design for a student who would close the app the moment it made him feel defined by his struggle. The product had to say "you're not alone" without ever saying "you're lonely." That constraint shaped everything.

what comes next

Signals is designed for the first 90 days. What happens after week 12 is intentionally unanswered - because by then, the app should no longer be the most important relationship in a student's life.

Three numbers would tell us if it worked.

Day 7 retention - does the habit survive the first week, or does Aarav open it twice and disappear?

Buddy connection rate - of every daily move completed, how many lead to a matched pair? This is the product's core bet: that action precedes friendship.

Wall-to-name ratio - how many students who read the signal wall anonymously eventually reveal who they are?

V2 explores city mapping, group challenges, and a senior mentor layer. But those come after one student has helped one other student find a laundromat, or sat somewhere new for lunch, or sent a message that started with "I felt that too."
